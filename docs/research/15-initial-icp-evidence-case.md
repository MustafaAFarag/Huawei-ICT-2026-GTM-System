# Caravan’s initial ICP: a decision from existing evidence

17 October 2026. Research only. This is an actionable initial market choice from private workflow evidence and public sources; interviews are not a prerequisite for making this choice now. No companies were contacted, no application/Figma changes were made, and no paid tools were activated.

## Decision

**Start with Egypt-based HR-outsourcing and payroll-service providers selling corporate services into Saudi Arabia and the wider Gulf, with a founder or BD manager directing a small sales team.** Include staffing/EOR providers where winning employer accounts is the job. Recruitment-only firms are adjacent when they need corporate client acquisition, rather than candidate sourcing or a replacement ATS.

The initial operational filter is **1–5 people working on business development**, an existing sellable service and corporate client evidence, and recurring account-list/outreach/follow-up work. This is a proposed filter informed by the four owner labels in the supplied workbook, not a measured market-wide headcount band. Do not use total employee count as the main filter: an outsourcing provider’s published headcount may include its deployed workforce.

This narrows the first acquisition and product-prioritisation focus within the existing founder-led-team/agency direction. It does not change the broader catalogue, agreed plans, Arabic support or Asset Studio decision.

**Positioning:** “Caravan helps HR-service sales teams turn prospect lists into researched Arabic/English outreach, organised follow-ups and a visible client pipeline.”

The intended outcome is more qualified client conversations with less coordination work. The current code still needs a real contact-provider and delivery/reply path before promising automated prospect discovery or end-to-end execution; see [audit 14](14-caravan-business-implementation-audit.md).

## The buyer, user and prospect are different

| Layer | Initial definition |
| --- | --- |
| Caravan’s customer | A provider selling outsourced HR, payroll, staffing or EOR services to other businesses |
| Economic buyer | Founder/managing director or Head of Business Development with purchasing authority |
| Daily user | BD executive, SDR or sales coordinator; manager reviews targeting and copy |
| That customer’s prospects | Employers, expanding firms, selected SMEs, partners or enterprise accounts, depending on its service and qualification rules |
| Job being purchased | Reduce the research, copy, handoff and follow-up work needed to build a corporate client pipeline |

A bank in the supplied ABM list is evidence about the HR provider’s target account, not evidence that banks are Caravan’s best software buyers. An in-house HR department primarily wants to manage people; our initial customer wants to sell HR services.

## Why this is the strongest starting case

### 1. The supplied files show the actual operating job

The current folder contains **40 files: 21 CSVs, 12 workbooks, three Markdown files and four other assets**. There are 39 distinct file hashes. Versions, exports and multiple campaign packs are not independent customer observations. These files describe one engagement/process context; they are much stronger evidence of workflow requirements than of market size or purchase intent.

The 160-row validation workbook provides these direct observations:

| Observation | What it supports | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Four distinct populated owner labels | Coordination and team handoffs matter | Four independently verified employees or a universal optimal team size |
| 106 rows marked Arabic + English; 54 English | Bilingual outreach is relevant to this engagement | A 66% bilingual requirement across the entire regional market |

The 78-row ABM sheet has 48 columns spanning research, evidence, account/person triggers, angles, multiple channels, confidence and follow-up logic. The pilot workbook has separate launch-gate, bilingual message, interview, capture and scorecard sheets. Together they justify a focused workflow product: **research → qualify → choose angle → prepare copy/profile → review → follow up → reconcile CRM/outcomes**.

## A practical ICP card

| Field | First qualification rule |
| --- | --- |
| Service | Corporate HR outsourcing, payroll, staffing or EOR; recruitment accepted when BD is the need |
| Geography | Egypt-based sales/operations; already serving or actively targeting GCC employers |
| Stage | Real service delivery and public/client evidence; not only a future business idea |
| Workload | Recurring named-account campaigns and follow-up work; an initial 50–200-record batch is a proposed operational fit, based on source batch sizes rather than market statistics |
| Team | Proposed 1–5 BD operators plus an accountable owner; verify the actual workflow, not the LinkedIn company-size band |
| Current process | Spreadsheets/CRM plus email, LinkedIn/manual calls; copy/research/status moves between tools |
| Buying trigger | Published BD hiring, Gulf-market expansion, a campaign/event list, new service line, or a visible initiative to organise sales |
| Buyer | Founder, managing director or BD head; an SDR is usually the user, not the budget owner |
| Success | Faster reviewed campaign preparation, fewer missed follow-ups, better-qualified conversations and accountable CRM records |
| Exclude/defer | Candidate-sourcing-only need; no sellable offer; no corporate sales activity; no supported contact/delivery path; enterprise replacement demands; unwilling to review claims or use the permitted channel |

Do not invent an exact minimum contract value or software budget. Prefer firms already investing in client acquisition and a service whose incremental gross profit could justify the subscription plus supplier costs; verify those inputs when they become available.

For public prospect qualification, store **service URL, geographic evidence, BD/workflow signal and date, buyer-role hypothesis, unresolved fields and confidence**. A “hiring” signal about a prospect's customer does not prove the provider will buy Caravan. Separate product-fit evidence from purchase timing, contact availability and communication permission. The website or a job advertisement provides no permission to send.

## Product and business case under the time constraint

We can choose this ICP now and make the demo tell one coherent story: **an HR-service BD team imports a prospect batch, resolves duplicates and missing context, chooses a service angle, prepares reviewed Arabic/English outreach and a company profile, then sees the next follow-up and recorded outcome.**

Priorities follow the supplied data:

1. Make qualification service-specific and bilingual. Separate persona, priority and HR setup; preserve unknowns.
2. Make one campaign carry the list, research, approved offer, drafts, owner and next action together. Import review and trustworthy statuses are central, not admin extras.
3. Prioritise the capability/profile one-pager, tailored introduction, meeting brief and proposal before additional unrelated format breadth. The `send_profile` source labels give a concrete reason for a sales asset; conversion lift is still unproven. Retain brand books as shared context.
4. Complete one supported contact/sender/reply handoff before describing the product as fully automated lead generation. Keep LinkedIn/calls explicit manual tasks where appropriate.
5. Show qualified conversations, meetings and follow-ups alongside time/credit cost. Record why an item was rejected and which version was used. This is the beginning of useful domain knowledge; it is not a proven moat yet.

For the financial case use a transparent worksheet: **monthly value = preparation/follow-up time actually saved × the customer’s loaded hourly cost + defensibly attributable incremental gross profit − added supplier costs**. Compare with the subscription. Until those inputs exist, do not substitute competitors’ success-story revenue or assume one new contract pays for everything. Public tool pricing demonstrates an established priced category, not what a particular prospect will pay.

The differentiation to test is a guided Egypt/Gulf HR-services sales workflow with reviewed bilingual copy, service-specific qualification, grounded capability assets and reliable handoffs. It must demonstrate less setup/coordination work than the customer’s existing stack. General chat, content generation, CRM consolidation or “AI-powered” by themselves are already offered elsewhere.

## Confidence, counterarguments and next decisions

- **High confidence:** the supplied engagement involves this operating workflow; similar regional HRO sales roles and providers are publicly observable.
- **Moderate confidence:** this is the best initial fit for Caravan’s current knowledge and implementation. The comparison is strategic judgement, not proof of demand across the whole sector.
- **Low/unresolved:** exact willingness to pay, supplier data coverage, switching willingness, acquisition cost, retention and sales lift. No public company is labelled a buyer merely because it has a website or sales role.

The strongest counterargument is that these firms already have an ATS/CRM plus Apollo/Smartlead, and an extra workspace adds work. A recruitment firm may value candidate delivery much more than corporate prospect preparation. That is why the initial choice centres HR/payroll providers’ **client-acquisition team** and why integration/manual handoff is preferable to demanding a full system replacement.

No interview dependency blocks the next product decision. Use the current source workflow as a de-identified acceptance scenario, make the supported capability boundary explicit, and choose this audience for positioning. When any real usage, payment or rejection arrives, update confidence and priorities. Do not label a desk-research decision as measured product-market fit.
